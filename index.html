Selected projects

Climate variability and suicide in Switzerland

MSc thesis — a multi-model analysis of explanatory and predictive power

This thesis asked how short-term weather variation relates to suicide rates, and whether that relationship is better captured by an explanatory model or a predictive one. I sourced and cleaned multi-source climate and suicide datasets, engineering lagged and rolling features so the models could pick up delayed effects rather than just same-day ones.

The core method was a Distributed Lag Non-Linear Model (DLNM), which is built to separate out effects that are non-linear and delayed at the same time — the shape in the hero graphic above is the kind of curve this method produces. I compared that against Random Forest and XGBoost models for predictive performance, and a Poisson regression on PCA-reduced climate variables for a more classical explanatory baseline. Models were evaluated using RMSE for predictive accuracy and AIC-based selection for model fit.

Mapping coffee phenology from space

Data Science Intern, Serasa Experian — São Paulo, Brazil

During a data science internship at Serasa Experian, I built a Python pipeline combining Landsat and MapBiomas satellite data to classify land cover and track the growth stages of coffee crops across the Caconde region. I trained and evaluated Random Forest and XGBoost classifiers, with most models exceeding 90% accuracy in identifying both land classes and crop growth stages.

The output was a hierarchical spatial classification map, delivered as a feasibility assessment for scaling this approach into automated, region-wide agricultural monitoring.

The code for this project was developed for internal use during the internship and isn't publicly available; the summary above reflects the published feasibility outcome.
